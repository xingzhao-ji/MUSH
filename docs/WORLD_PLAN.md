# MUSH world plan (landscape, climate, culture)

Status: planning document. It separates three things:
- **User direction:** what the user asked for.
- **Proposal:** suggestions for discussion, not choices the user has made.
- **Built:** what exists in the prototype now, with evidence in `docs/VERIFICATION.md`.

Nothing here claims an unbuilt region exists.

## User direction (recorded)
- **Not plain green plains:** the user wants magnificent scenery inspired by the world's most renowned natural landscapes. Yosemite, Yellowstone and Iceland are examples, not limits.
- **Breadth:** glaciers and globally famous landscapes, including alpine and glacial lakes, broad cascades, limestone lagoons and karst, volcanic coasts, tuff towers, canyon reveals, geothermal terraces, fjords and narrow river canyons.
- **Regional identity:** architectural styles, hot-air balloons, major falls, different colours and weather. Different parts of the world should have different seasons.
- **Culture:** carefully articulated, invented and coherent; real cultures must not be treated as interchangeable costumes.

## Organising principle (proposal)
One continuous world, organised by elevation and watersheds. Ice feeds falls, falls feed the fungal valley, and the valley drains to wetlands and a coast. Passages between regions follow the water: gorges, passes and river terraces. Each region has authored climate states and weather. This is a regional identity, not a calendar simulation; time-driven seasons are a later option.

## Culture layer (proposal; invented, nothing built)
Ecology shapes materials, routes, food, crafts and gathering places. The mushroom kingdoms are linked by the water that runs between them.
- **High Ice Basin communities:**
  - **Routes and craft:** keep the seasonal meltwater routes and cairn waymarks, and practise dry-stone craft.
  - **Architecture:** low corbelled stone halls set into granite benches, with turf roofs and wind walls.
  - **Tradition:** a ritual of "first melt", when the falls wake.
  - **Food:** they trade cold-cured fungus.
- **Granite Falls Valley (the fungal heartland):**
  - **Knowledge:** keeps flood knowledge, with marks on the rock at past high-water lines.
  - **Gathering:** shared water customs at the plunge pools.
  - **Architecture:** timber and mycelium-felt lodges raised on stone plinths above flood level.
  - **Balloons:** spore-silk hot-air balloons ride the valley's dawn calm and evening updrafts along the warm granite walls. A festival of lanterns and balloons marks the return of the warm season.
- **Downstream wetlands and coast:**
  - **Craft:** reed and mycelium craft.
  - **Travel:** ferry exchange along the lagoons.
  - **Architecture:** stilted halls and woven causeways.
  - **Role:** the meeting place between kingdoms; their markets trade valley spore-silk for coastal salt and dyes.
- **Relationships:** upstream and downstream depend on one another. The ice communities send water warnings, the valley sends balloons and the coast sends salt and news. Disputes are about water timing, not territory.
- **Guardrails:** sacred symbols, dress and architectural motifs of real living cultures are not borrowed. Histories are invented to fit the ecology.

## Near-term build order (environment first)
1. **Built and in progress:** one dominant landmark, the West Wall and Falls, with the High Ice Basin source above the current valley. Its evidence and limitations are in `docs/VERIFICATION.md`.
2. **Adjacent transitions:** the plunge basin outflow to the valley lake, shore and ground transitions, and wet rock and spray.
3. **Reserved, not built:** settlement footprints on the granite benches and plunge-pool terraces; passages along the falls' drainage and the lake outlet; a few distant balloon silhouettes as environmental accents, only once the landform reads well.
4. **Later regions:** following the watershed order above, one at a time, each with its own authored weather state.

Not now: buildings, citizens, diplomacy or kingdom systems, calendar seasons, or wholesale biome expansion.
