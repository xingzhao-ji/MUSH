# Story interaction text

Status: **proposal, not built**. These are the complete short prompts for the eighteen external story events named in [the manifest](story_manifest.json). They supplement the character dialogue; they are not engine scripts. A future world interaction owns observation, placement, loan props and event flags according to [SCHEMA.md](dialogue/SCHEMA.md). Selecting a promise in dialogue never performs these actions.

## Shared behavior and text

All interactions have **“Back”**. Back preserves acknowledged progress and does not set an unfinished objective's event. Ordinary walking continues to carry a held loan. Explicitly abandoning the carry task, closing the session or choosing to return the loan puts it back at its accessible source stand; the player can take it again. Nothing can be lost, sold, consumed or dropped into unreachable terrain. Loan props appear at the named starting stand after quest acceptance and remain available after preparation. They are distinct world objects; completion souvenirs belong to the proposed quest pouch. Neither uses the player's six furniture places. Carrying does not slow movement, disable Float, drain magic or prevent menus. A future implementer must build and test these rules; they are not current features.

Shared unavailable prompt: **“Let's hear what our neighbor needs first.”** After acceptance but before preparation: **“Visit the marked place, then talk with your neighbor.”** After the objective was observed: **“Ready to show your neighbor.”** Optional return prompt: **“Leave this copy here for another visit?”** Choices: **“Leave the copy”** / **“Keep carrying”**. The original source copy always remains at its stand. No costs, countdowns, progress percentages or failure alarms appear.

Every event is latched only once its prerequisite quest is accepted, its preparation is acknowledged, and the listed objective is physically satisfied. Main events instead require their corresponding main quest accepted. Existing suitable garden/furniture arrangements count when shown. An object's world placement is an intention until its destination validates it. All routes below are ordinary ground-level proposals requiring later reachability verification. Float may offer a different view; no task requires it.
